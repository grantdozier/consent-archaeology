    WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING

    Every company listed on the left publishes a record matching the identity you verified. We list their legal name, headquarters address, and state registry ID, because it turns out companies have addresses too, and unlike you, they filed theirs voluntarily. With the state. To sell yours.

    The buttons generate letters. That is all they do. We do not send them — you do, as yourself, from your own inbox, because an anonymous demand is legally void: the company is entitled to verify who is asking. Your name at the bottom is what makes the letter work.

    • RIGHT TO KNOW — every specific piece of data they hold on you. Cal. Civ. Code §1798.110.
    • DISCLOSURE TRAIL — everyone they sold or shared it with. §1798.115.
    • DELETION — a demand that they delete your record, which the law lets you make without explaining yourself. §1798.105.
    • CONSENT PROVENANCE ★ — the headline act. GDPR Art. 7(1) says a company must be able to demonstrate you consented. This letter asks for the date, the exact form you saw, how they verified it was you, and the chain of custody if they bought the record. For data acquired third-hand in 2016, many companies simply do not have this. The letter asks anyway. In writing. With a deadline.

    We are not lawyers, this is not legal advice, and no outcome is promised. These are self-help documents — like a recipe, if the recipe were furious.

    WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING

    This is a timestamped log of what happened to you, with each entry hashed and chained to the previous one so nobody — including us — can quietly edit history later. When you submit, the server returns the entry's hash; that hash is your tamper-evidence.

    Element 4 is the one nobody else systematically collects: the ticket history that vanished, the support bot that looped you for six weeks, the documentation that existed right up until you needed it. One person's version of that story is an anecdote. Ten thousand consistent, timestamped, exportable versions of it is a pattern a law firm can put in front of an arbitrator.

    Which is the point: mass arbitration works precisely because these companies wrote arbitration clauses to prevent class actions. Every clean, consistent, well-evidenced individual file raises the cost of "we lost the record" by one filing fee. Yours is one of them. Export it whenever you like — it is your file, not ours.
