This is a static web page. The glow is CSS. The "secure facility" is free hosting on GitHub Pages, which is also where programmers keep their dotfiles.

Your connection is encrypted with ordinary HTTPS — the same encryption you used the last time you ordered a burrito online. We are dramatizing the padlock icon. The padlock was already there.

What this site actually does: data brokers sell records about you — name, address history, phone, relatives, an income guess — for about $0.003. We check a fixed, human-reviewed list of those brokers for a record matching you (and only you — you have to prove your identity by email first). Then we help you send each one a letter with a very specific question: show me the record proving I agreed to this. Under GDPR Article 7(1) they are required to be able to. A remarkable number of them cannot.

Everything is free. There is a donation button at the very end, after your results, and if you dismiss it we never ask again.

WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING

This is a form. Here is what every field is for, because forms that collect personal data and don't explain themselves are the whole reason this site exists:

  • Full name — brokers index people by name plus address history, so we search with what they'd have. Yes, giving a privacy tool your name is uncomfortable. It's encrypted at rest, it appears in no logs, and it is automatically deleted after 90 days of inactivity.
  • Email — we send one verification link to it. Clicking that link is the only thing separating this site from a people-search engine for stalkers, so it's not optional and there is no admin override. We checked. There is no admin.
  • Phone — optional. Improves matching. Skip it if it feels weird. It feels a little weird to us too.
  • City / state — brokers file you geographically. "John Smith" is forty thousand people. "John Smith, Lafayette, LA" is a shelf.
  • Prior addresses — optional, but this is the good stuff. Nothing in this universe remembers your 2014 apartment as fondly as a data broker does.
  • Birth year — optional, disambiguation only. Just the year. We are not a candle company.
  • SSN — there is no SSN field. There will never be an SSN field. A public-source website collecting SSNs behind dramatic lighting is a phishing kit no matter who wrote it, and brokers don't need an SSN to find you anyway. The amber box points at the thing that actually protects your SSN, which is a credit freeze, which is free.

When you press the button, exactly one HTTPS POST goes to our server. It stores the form (encrypted), asks Brevo to email you a verification link, and then nothing further happens until you click that link. The progress bar below the button tracks that one real request — it moves when the request settles and at no other time.
