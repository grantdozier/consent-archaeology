> **Framing note.** This file does not allege that Twilio acted unlawfully or in bad
> faith, and it should not be read as doing so. The documented point is narrower, and
> for this project's purposes stronger: **a dispute of roughly $20,000 turned on an
> incident that neither party could locate the source of, and the support process
> produced no durable record that either side could later examine.** That is
> element 4 (see [`DESIGN.md`](../DESIGN.md) §4c). The pattern, not the accusation,
> is the point.

## Summary

Twilio discontinued service on the claimant's account. The bill at issue was
approximately $20,000. The stated reason for the discontinuation was **suspicious
activity**. In the exchanges that followed, the source of the flagged incident could
not be located — **by either party**: the claimant could not identify it in his own
systems and usage records, and Twilio's support process did not produce it either.
The support process ended without resolving the dispute.

## Documentation that later became unavailable

The central documented fact of this case: **the incident that triggered the
discontinuation could not be sourced by either party.** The claimant searched his own
systems and records and could not locate its origin; the support process on Twilio's
side likewise did not produce the underlying incident record, the detection basis, or
an examinable account of what the "suspicious activity" was.

Whatever record the shutoff decision was based on, neither side could later put it on
the table. A $20,000 outcome rested on evidence unavailable to both the party who
generated the traffic and the party who flagged it.
